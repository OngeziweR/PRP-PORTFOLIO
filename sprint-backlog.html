My Personal Reflection

Working with the Sprint Backlog helped me understand how a large project can be divided into smaller and more manageable stages. Instead of trying to complete the entire Second-Hand Textbook Swap project at once, the team organised the work into different sprints and assigned specific tasks to each stage.

I learned that having a clear Sprint Backlog makes it easier to understand what needs to be completed during a particular sprint. The Trello board helped me see the tasks that were planned, follow the progress of the work, and understand how my tasks contributed to the overall project.

One of the things I found valuable was seeing how different tasks were connected. For example, the project moved from business planning and requirements gathering to system design, development, testing and final preparation. Breaking these activities into sprints made the project feel more organised and manageable.

My contribution to the sprint activities included working on requirements, system design, documentation, testing and other project tasks. This helped me develop a better understanding of how individual contributions support the progress of the whole team.

Overall, the Sprint Backlog improved my understanding of Agile development and showed me the importance of planning, organisation and teamwork. If I worked on a similar project again, I would continue using sprint-based planning because it provides a clear structure for managing a large project and monitoring progress.
