Agile Logbook

This Agile Logbook documents the progress of the Second-Hand Textbook Swap project from its beginning to its completion. It summarises the project's main stages, my contributions, the use of Agile practices, and the lessons I learned throughout the process.

Project Overview

Project: Second-Hand Textbook Swap

Start Date: 23 July 2026

Completion Date: 24 October 2026

Development Approach: Agile

Project Structure: 7 sprints

Project Timeline and Progress

The project ran from 23 July to 24 October 2026. Work was organised into seven sprints, with each sprint covering a different stage of the project. The timeline below summarises the planned work represented in our Product Backlog.

Sprint 1: Project Initiation and Business Planning

  • Defined the business idea and problem statement.
  • Identified stakeholders and the target market.
  • Conducted market and competitor research.
  • Completed the SWOT analysis and value proposition.

Sprint 2: Business Planning

  • Developed the mission and vision statements.
  • Defined business goals and objectives.
  • Prepared the business model and marketing strategy.
  • Worked on operational, financial and risk planning.

Sprint 3: Requirements and System Design

  • Gathered functional and non-functional requirements.
  • Created user stories for buyers and sellers.
  • Contributed to architecture and database design.
  • Worked on the ERD and use case diagram.

Sprint 4: User Interface Design

  • Designed the main page and registration interface.
  • Planned the user dashboard and textbook listings.
  • Worked on search, filtering and messaging interfaces.
  • Reviewed and finalised the prototype design.

Sprint 5: Application Development

  • Planned development of registration and login features.
  • Worked on profile management and textbook listings.
  • Included search, filtering and messaging functionality.
  • Addressed frontend and backend integration tasks.

Sprint 6: Testing and Improvement

  • Planned testing of registration and login.
  • Tested textbook listings and search functionality.
  • Addressed bugs and performance improvements.
  • Compiled the user acceptance testing report.

Sprint 7: Finalisation and Submission

  • Completed and reviewed project documentation.
  • Prepared the user manual and final presentation.
  • Prepared the live demonstration.
  • Reviewed and submitted the final project.

Note: The sprint descriptions summarise the planned project stages. Separate start and end dates for each sprint were not recorded in the available project information.

My Contributions Throughout the Project

I contributed to different stages of the project, including business planning, research, requirements gathering, system design, testing and documentation. My responsibilities included:

  • Helping define the business idea and problem.
  • Researching the target market and competitors.
  • Contributing to the SWOT analysis and value proposition.
  • Documenting business goals and customer segments.
  • Recording functional and usability requirements.
  • Writing user stories for buyers and sellers.
  • Contributing to database and system design activities.
  • Uploading wireframes to the project documentation.
  • Contributing to performance improvements and user acceptance testing.
  • Proofreading technical documentation and preparing final deliverables.

Use of Agile Practices

Our team organised the project into seven sprints, with each sprint containing a group of related tasks. We used Trello to organise task cards, view the work planned for each sprint and monitor project activities.

The Product Backlog provided an overview of the work required for the project, while the Sprint Backlog focused on tasks organised within individual sprints. This structure helped make the workload easier to understand and manage.

The project records available for this portfolio include the Trello backlog evidence and documentation of project contributions. A formal dated log of individual meetings and daily activities was not kept.

Project Completion and Outcome

The project was completed on 24 October 2026. The final stage included reviewing the system, preparing the technical documentation and user manual, preparing the presentation and demonstration, and submitting the project.

The project gave me experience in planning a software project, contributing to different development stages, working with a team and organising tasks through an Agile approach.

My Personal Reflection

Working on the Second-Hand Textbook Swap project helped me understand how Agile principles can be applied to a group project. Dividing the work into seven sprints made a large project easier to approach because the team could focus on smaller activities at different stages.

I learned the importance of planning and organising tasks so that the team could understand what needed to be done and what work remained. Trello helped me view the tasks assigned to each sprint, follow the project's organisation and understand how different activities contributed to the final outcome.

My contributions across business planning, research, requirements, design, testing and documentation also helped me understand that successful project delivery involves more than developing the application. Research, communication, documentation and testing are important parts of the overall process.

One limitation of our project records was that we did not maintain a formal dated log of meetings and daily progress. Looking back, I would keep a regular logbook in a future project, recording completed activities, decisions, challenges and lessons learned. This would make it easier to demonstrate how the project progressed over time.

Overall, this project strengthened my understanding of Agile planning, task management and teamwork. It showed me the value of breaking a large project into smaller stages and keeping project evidence organised throughout the development process.
