Product Backlog Overview

The Product Backlog for the Second-Hand Textbook Swap project contains the planned work required to develop the project from the initial business idea through to system development, testing, documentation, and final presentation.

The backlog was managed using Trello and organised according to sprints and project days. The backlog shows the progression of the project from business planning and requirements gathering to design, development, testing, documentation, and finalisation.

Evidence Explanation

The Product Backlog demonstrates how the project work was planned and organised throughout the development process. The backlog contains activities covering business planning, requirements gathering, system design, user interface design, development, testing, documentation, and final presentation preparation.

Organising the work into sprints made it possible for the team to divide the larger project into smaller stages. Each stage focused on a specific area of the project and provided a sequence for completing the required work.

The Trello board also provided a central location where the team could monitor project tasks, identify completed work, and keep track of the project's progress.

The Product Backlog therefore provided the team with a structured view of the work that needed to be completed from project initiation through to final submission.

Reflection

My Reflection

Working on the Product Backlog for the Second-Hand Textbook Swap project helped me understand the importance of planning, organisation, and research in project development. My contribution covered several stages of the project, including defining the business idea, researching the target market and competitors, identifying stakeholders, preparing business planning documents, gathering requirements, contributing to system design, improving application performance, conducting user acceptance testing, and preparing the final documentation and presentation. One of the most important things I learned was the importance of organising work so that the team could clearly identify what had been completed and what still needed to be done. I also learned that researching a project thoroughly helps me gain a better understanding of the problem, the target users, and the solution being developed. This research helped me contribute to different aspects of the project, from understanding students' textbook needs to identifying customer segments and defining the platform's value proposition. Dividing the project into 54 backlog items across seven sprints made the workload easier to manage. It gave every team member an opportunity to participate and contribute to the project, while making the overall workload more manageable. Instead of approaching the project as one large task, we could work through smaller activities and gradually progress towards completing the final product. Trello played an important role in organising the Product Backlog. It helped me identify the work that needed to be done, track completed tasks, see what my teammates were working on, and keep the project organised. Having the backlog in one place made it easier to follow the project's progress and understand how individual tasks contributed to the overall project. I did not experience any major challenges while working with the Product Backlog. The organisation of the tasks and the opportunity for team members to participate helped us work through the planned activities. This experience also taught me that Agile development allows a large project to be developed gradually by completing smaller pieces of work across different sprints. If I were to work on a similar project again, I would continue using the same approach to organising tasks, conducting research, and tracking progress. Overall, this experience strengthened my understanding of Product Backlogs, teamwork, task management, and the importance of planning throughout the software development lifecycle.
